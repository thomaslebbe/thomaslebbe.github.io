Working Papers

The Elasticity of Substitution in Energy
with Freddy Heylen · UGent FEB Working Paper 26/1140
We estimate the elasticity of substitution between clean and dirty energy for 13 OECD countries (1980–2020). We extend the CES framework of León-Ledesma, originally built for capital and labour, to clean and dirty energy. Accounting for technology and infrastructure explains the broad range of estimates in the literature. The elasticity is well below one when technology and infrastructure are held constant. It rises to between 2 and 3 when they respond to prices. Substitution is thus not a one-way street: prices and technology interact.

The Energy Transition in Long-Run Macroeconomics
2026
This paper builds the theory behind these interactions. I develop a structural general equilibrium model of the long-run energy transition. Technology is endogenous. It responds to fossil fuel prices, public infrastructure investment, and carbon taxes. This shifts how technology grows and how clean energy prices evolve. The model is estimated on historical data. Both public infrastructure and fossil fuel prices are needed; it is not one or the other. The same holds for capital: public and private capital both play a role.

Heat Distributed Unequally in the Sovereign Bond Market
with Azzam Santosa · 2026
We study how heat shocks are priced in sovereign bond markets. Heat shocks are temperature anomalies with a low mean and a high variance. This sets them apart from the disaster risk studied in earlier work. We combine a quantitative sovereign default model with panel regressions for 76 countries (1995–2017). Heat shocks widen bond and CDS spreads and raise default probabilities. The effects are strongest in emerging markets with large agricultural sectors.

Work in Progress

Green Fiscal Policy in a High-Debt, Ageing Society
working title
The two papers above tell a supply-side story. This paper adds the demand side. I introduce households that differ in income and energy demand. The model also includes high public debt and an ageing population. These features complicate the policy debate. The goal is a framework that speaks to real policy choices.
