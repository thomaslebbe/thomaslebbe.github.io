About

I am a macroeconomist fascinated by one question: how can economies decarbonize without undermining their public finances? The energy transition is often studied as a technological problem, but it is just as much a fiscal one — carbon taxes, green subsidies and public investment all run through government budgets that are already under pressure from debt and ageing.

I pursue this question as a PhD researcher at Ghent University, where I held a competitive FWO doctoral fellowship (2021–2025). I build structural general equilibrium models — DSGE and OLG — of climate policy and the energy transition, and combine them with panel-econometric work on energy prices and energy-mix dynamics to keep the models honest about the data.

I hold a Research Master's in Economics from UCLouvain (cum laude, 2021) and an MSc in Economics from Ghent University (magna cum laude, 2019). My PhD defence is expected in 2027.

Outreach & Engagement

I host a podcast for the economics department of Ghent University, translating policy-focused economics research into an accessible format for a broad audience.

I was twice elected to the faculty board, where I advocated better support for PhD students with little or no funding.

For fifteen years I volunteered in Bruges with Oranje vzw, an organisation that works toward an open and inclusive society for people with disabilities — a cause I still care deeply about.

Research Interests

  • Fiscal policy and public debt sustainability
